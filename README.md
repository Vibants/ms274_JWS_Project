## Project Outline/Plan
For this remote sensing project, I'll investigate how satellite-derived sea surface temperature (SST) and chlorophyll-a (Chl-a) concentrations relate to the fine-scale horizontal movement and habitat use of juvenile white sharks (JWS; Carcharodon carcharias) at New Brighton Beach in Monterey Bay, California. Monterey Bay represents an important northern seasonal habitat for juvenile white sharks, where localized oceanographic conditions may influence their spatial distribution and residency.

This project will integrate satellite observations with in-situ acoustic telemetry data collected from a receiver array deployed on May 22, 2026, with retrieval anticipated in mid-November 2026. Satellite-derived SST and Chl-a will be used to characterize seasonal environmental variability throughout the deployment period, while acoustic detections will be used to evaluate shark residency, horizontal displacement, and spatial habitat use.This project will explore whether changes in thermal conditions and surface productivity are associated with variation in JWS movement and habitat use within a dynamic coastal environment.
### Project Objectives
Objective 1:
The first objective is to quantify spatial and temporal changes in satellite-derived SST and Chl-a concentrations around New Brighton Beach from May 22 through mid-November 2026. This analysis will characterize seasonal warming and cooling patterns, identify relatively warm surface waters and spatial temperature gradients, and examine changes in Chl-a concentration as an indicator of phytoplankton biomass. They'll be given to identifying periods of elevated or reduced Chl-a and potential coastal productivity events throughout the deployment period.

Objective 2:
The second objective is to characterize the fine-scale spatial behavior of JWS using acoustic telemetry detections collected within the New Brighton Beach receiver array. Individual detection histories will be used to quantify residency duration, detection frequency, and temporal patterns in habitat occupancy. Where sufficient acoustic positioning data are available, horizontal movement and displacement will be estimated to identify areas of concentrated JWS activity and examine differences in spatial habitat use throughout the study period.

Objective 3:
The third objective is to integrate satellite-derived SST and Chl-a observations with acoustic telemetry measurements to investigate potential environmental influences on JWS habitat use. This analysis will examine whether warmer surface temperatures correspond to changes in residency, horizontal displacement, or spatial distribution. Additionally, variation in Chl-a concentration will be evaluated in relation to shark movement patterns, with consideration of potential time lags between environmental changes and observed behavioral responses.
### Study Area and Timeline
The primary study location is New Brighton Beach, along the northern coastline of Monterey Bay, California. The study will encompass the period from May 22, 2026, through mid-November 2026, corresponding to the deployment and anticipated retrieval of the acoustic receiver array. The array is designed to monitor fine-scale movements of JWS within a nearshore environment influenced by localized warming, coastal upwelling, and seasonal oceanographic variability.

Satellite-derived SST and Chl-a observations will be extracted over the same deployment period to establish temporal correspondence between remotely sensed environmental conditions and in-situ acoustic detections. The final end date will be updated once receiver retrieval is conducted.
### Planned Figures
The first figure will illustrate temporal changes in satellite-derived sea surface temperature (SST) near New Brighton Beach throughout the May–November 2026 acoustic deployment period. A time-series plot will characterize seasonal warming and cooling patterns, identify periods of elevated surface temperatures, and provide an environmental baseline for observing relationships between thermal variability and JWS habitat use.

The second figure will illustrate temporal changes in satellite-derived chlorophyll-a (Chl-a) concentrations near New Brighton Beach throughout the acoustic deployment period. A time-series plot will characterize seasonal fluctuations in surface phytoplankton biomass, identify periods of elevated or reduced Chl-a concentrations, and provide an environmental baseline for evaluating whether coastal productivity is associated with JWS habitat use.

The third figure will examine the relationship between satellite-derived SST and JWS horizontal movement within the New Brighton Beach acoustic receiver array. A scatterplot will compare SST observations with corresponding shark movement or habitat-use metrics, such as horizontal displacement, residency duration, or spatial occupancy. This analysis will investigate whether JWS exhibit greater habitat use or reduced horizontal displacement under relatively warmer surface conditions, potentially indicating thermal habitat preference or attraction to localized warm-water environments.

The fourth figure will examine the relationship between satellite-derived Chl-a concentrations and JWS horizontal movement within the acoustic receiver array. A scatterplot will compare Chl-a observations with corresponding shark movement or habitat-use metrics to investigate whether changes in surface productivity are associated with differences in residency, horizontal displacement, or spatial occupancy. This analysis will explore whether JWS exhibit greater habitat use in areas or periods characterized by elevated Chl-a concentrations, potentially reflecting indirect ecological relationships between primary productivity, prey availability, and shark habitat selection.
### Expected Outcomes
This project is expected to produce a reproducible analysis of seasonal SST and Chl-a variability in Monterey Bay and an initial assessment of how these environmental conditions relate to JWS habitat use. Satellite observations are expected to reveal seasonal changes in surface temperature and Chl-a distribution, including periods of localized warming and variable coastal productivity. The integration of acoustic telemetry data will provide information on JWS residency, horizontal movement, and spatial habitat use throughout the deployment period. Comparing these biological observations with satellite-derived environmental measurements may reveal associations between thermal variability, surface productivity, and shark movement behavior. The significance of these relationships will be determined by the observations. The expected outcome will be the development of a reproducible workflow that integrates remote sensing and in-situ acoustic telemetry data for coastal ecological research. The resulting datasets, figures, and analyses will provide a foundation for investigating environmental influences on JWS habitat use.
